# The name/alias benchmark

What comparing a name beside its list of aliases buys, measured on data where people are known under several names.
No public deduplication dataset carries alias lists, so this one is synthetic, generated by [`generate.py`](generate.py) from one seed.
It is a cpplink-only benchmark: splink has no comparison of this shape to hold it against.

## The data

Each person has a main identity (first name, surname, date of birth, postcode) and may have others:

- the nicknames of their first name, from a table of real English nicknames, including the collisions (`ted` is both an `edward` and a `theodore`, `kit` both a `katherine` and a `christopher`);
- a second surname, for 30% of people;
- a second date of birth, for 10%: the day and month swapped where that is a date, otherwise the year off by one.

A record is one sighting of a person.
It presents one identity in its main columns (a nickname 40% of the time, the second surname or date half the time) and lists some of the others in `first_name_aliases`, `surname_aliases` and `dob_aliases`.
70% of records come from a source that lists aliases at all, and such a source lists each of the person's other names with probability 0.6.
Names take a one-edit typo 5% of the time, values go missing at 1-10% per column, and a person moves postcode 20% of the time.
Surnames are Zipf-distributed (exponent 1.05 over 8,000 values), so term frequency has something to say.
The default run is 60,000 people, 108,021 records and 67,390 true pairs.

Every rate is a parameter of `generate.py` and was chosen by hand, not fitted to a real register.
Read the numbers below as what the mechanism does on data of this shape, not as what it will do on yours.

## What is compared

[`run.py`](run.py) holds everything fixed but one thing per row.

| Variant | Comparisons | Term frequency | Blocking |
|---|---|---|---|
| `scalar` | each name alone: `null, exact, fuzzy, fuzzy, else` | off | main columns |
| `scalar_tf` | the same | on | main columns |
| `alias` | each name beside its aliases: `null, exact, list_contains, fuzzy, contains_fuzzy, fuzzy, else` | off | main columns |
| `alias_tf` | the same | on | main columns |
| `alias_tf_keys` | the same | on | main columns plus one key per field |

The fuzzy levels are Jaro-Winkler 0.92 and 0.80 for the names and Levenshtein 1 and 2 for the date, which is compared as an ISO string because the membership levels read strings.
Postcode is `null, exact, levenshtein <= 1, else` in every variant.
The main-column blocking is `exact_value` on `dob` and `postcode` and `rare_value` on `surname` at 200.
A key is the smallest element of the main value and its aliases, written into the file and declared with `derived_from` on both columns, so a record filed under `bill` and one filed under `william` with `bill` among its aliases block together; the name keys are `rare_value` at 200 and the date key `exact_value`.

Quality is pairwise F1 over the transitive closure of the clustering, scored by [`../score.py`](../score.py), and the best over a 13-threshold sweep is reported, since moving the model moves its operating point.

## Results

Seed 20261119, 8 threads:

| Variant | Blocking recall | Candidates | Best F1 | Precision | Recall | at p >= |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `scalar` | 0.9654 | 2,440,956 | 0.9218 | 0.9658 | 0.8817 | 0.5 |
| `scalar_tf` | 0.9654 | 2,440,956 | 0.9464 | 0.9727 | 0.9214 | 0.5 |
| `alias` | 0.9654 | 2,440,956 | 0.9599 | 0.9865 | 0.9347 | 0.7 |
| `alias_tf` | 0.9654 | 2,440,956 | **0.9676** | 0.9840 | 0.9517 | 0.5 |
| `alias_tf_keys` | 0.9806 | 3,545,373 | **0.9712** | 0.9825 | 0.9602 | 0.5 |

Seeds 1 and 2 move every F1 by at most 0.003 and keep the order.

- **The alias levels are worth +0.038 F1 without term frequency and +0.021 with it**, mostly in recall: a nickname or a second surname that reads as a disagreement under scalar comparison reads as agreement through the other record's list.
- **Term frequency on the shape's exact level is worth +0.008 F1** (`alias` to `alias_tf`), against +0.025 on the scalar comparisons; the alias levels already recover some of the pairs that term frequency was rescuing.
- **The alias keys raise blocking recall from 96.5% to 98.1%** for 45% more candidates, and F1 by 0.004: the extra pairs are the ones filed under a different main name, and the alias levels are what lets them score.

## Running it

```bash
python bench/alias/generate.py --out bench/data/alias
python bench/alias/run.py --data bench/data/alias --out bench/results/alias --threads 8
```

The run takes about 20 seconds and writes each variant's schema, model, predictions and clusters, plus `summary.json` with the full threshold sweep.
